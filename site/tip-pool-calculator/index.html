How a tip pool by hours works

All the tips for the shift go into one pool. The pool is divided by the total hours worked to get a tip rate per hour, and each person gets that rate times their own hours. Someone who worked 8 hours gets twice what someone who worked 4 hours gets.

Example

$850 in tips and four people who worked 8, 6.5, 5 and 4 hours (23.5 hours total). The rate is $850 ÷ 23.5 = $36.17 per hour. The 8-hour shift takes $289.36 and the 4-hour shift takes $144.68.

Tip-out percentage

Some restaurants only pool part of the tips, for example servers tip out 20% to the support staff. Put that percent in "Percent of tips that go to the pool" and enter the hours of the support staff only.

Rules to keep in mind

Under the US Fair Labor Standards Act, managers and supervisors cannot keep employees' tips, and a pool that includes back-of-house staff has extra rules when the employer takes a tip credit. State laws vary, so check your state's labor department before setting up a policy.

Frequently asked questions

What is a fair way to split tips?

Splitting by hours worked is the most common and easiest to explain. Some teams also use points by role (for example server 1.0, busser 0.5).

Can managers be part of the tip pool?

Under federal law, managers and supervisors cannot keep any part of employees' tips. Check your state rules too.

Does this store my staff data?

No. Everything is calculated in your browser and nothing is sent or saved.
