How the car payment is calculated

Amount financed = price + sales tax + fees − down payment − trade-in. The monthly payment then uses the standard loan formula with your APR and term. In most states sales tax is charged only on the price minus the trade-in; a few (such as California, Michigan and Virginia) tax the full price, so switch that option to "No".

Example

A $32,000 car with $4,000 down, 7% tax and $800 in fees. You finance $31,040. At 7.5% for 60 months the payment is about $622 and you pay about $6,280 in interest.

Longer is not cheaper

A 72 or 84-month loan lowers the payment but adds a lot of interest, and you can owe more than the car is worth for years (being "upside down"). A popular guideline is 20/4/10: at least 20% down, no more than 4 years, and total car costs under 10% of your income.

Get pre-approved first

Ask your bank or a credit union for a pre-approval before visiting the dealer. Then you can compare their APR with the dealer's offer and negotiate the price of the car separately from the financing.

Frequently asked questions

What is a good APR for a car loan?

It depends on your credit score and whether the car is new or used. Borrowers with excellent credit get the lowest rates; used cars and lower scores pay more. Compare at least two offers.

Should I include sales tax in the loan?

You can pay it upfront to borrow less, or roll it into the loan. Rolling it in is common but you pay interest on it.

Is it better to pay cash or finance?

If the loan APR is higher than what your savings earn, paying cash (or a larger down payment) saves money. Keep an emergency fund first.
