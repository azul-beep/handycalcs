The food cost formula

Food cost % = (beginning inventory + purchases − ending inventory) ÷ food sales × 100. The part in parentheses is the cost of goods sold (COGS): what you actually used.

Example

You start the month with $8,000 of inventory, buy $12,500 and end with $7,200. You used $13,300. With $43,000 in food sales, the food cost is 13,300 ÷ 43,000 = 30.9%.

How to lower your food cost

  1. Count inventory on the same day and time every period.
  2. Standardize recipes and portion sizes, and train the line on them.
  3. Track waste: spoiled food, mistakes and staff meals.
  4. Compare supplier prices every quarter.
  5. Re-price dishes whose plate cost went up (use the menu price calculator).

Frequently asked questions

How often should I calculate food cost?

Weekly is best for catching problems early; monthly is the minimum.

Should beverages be included?

Track beverage and alcohol cost separately; they have very different targets (often 18%–24% for alcohol).

What is prime cost?

Food and beverage cost plus total labor cost. A common target is 60% of sales or less.
