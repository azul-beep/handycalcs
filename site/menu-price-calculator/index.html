The menu pricing formula

Menu price = plate cost ÷ target food cost %. A plate that costs $4.20 in ingredients with a 30% target sells for $4.20 ÷ 0.30 = $14.00. Your gross profit on that plate is $9.80 before labor and rent.

How to get the plate cost

List every ingredient in the recipe with the amount used and its purchase price, and add them up. Include small things (oil, garnish, sauce, bread) and divide batch recipes by the number of portions. Update it when suppliers change prices.

What food cost percentage to use

  • 25%–28%: pizza, pasta, breakfast and other cheap-ingredient dishes.
  • 28%–32%: typical full-service restaurant target.
  • 32%–40%: steaks and seafood, where a higher cost is accepted for a higher dollar profit.

Look at the whole menu: a low-cost appetizer can balance a premium main course.

Frequently asked questions

What is a good food cost percentage?

Most restaurants aim for 28% to 35% overall. Fast casual is often lower, fine dining with expensive proteins can be higher.

What is the difference between markup and food cost?

Food cost is cost divided by price (30%). Markup is how much you add on top of cost (233% in the same example).

Should prices end in .99?

Charm pricing (.99 or .95) is common in casual places. Upscale menus often use whole numbers without the dollar sign.
