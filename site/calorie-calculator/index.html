How the calories are estimated

First the calculator finds your basal metabolic rate (BMR), the energy your body uses at rest, with the Mifflin-St Jeor equation, one of the most accurate for most adults:

  • Men: 10 × weight (kg) + 6.25 × height (cm) − 5 × age + 5
  • Women: 10 × weight (kg) + 6.25 × height (cm) − 5 × age − 161

Then it multiplies the BMR by an activity factor (1.2 for sedentary up to 1.9 for very active) to get your total daily energy expenditure, or TDEE: the calories that keep your weight stable.

Losing or gaining weight

About 500 calories a day under your TDEE leads to roughly 1 pound a week of weight loss for many people; 250 calories gives about half a pound. Real results vary. The calculator never suggests going below 1,200 calories a day for women or 1,500 for men without medical supervision.

Tips

  • Track for two or three weeks and adjust: if your weight does not move, change the number by 100–200 calories.
  • Protein, fiber and vegetables help you feel full on fewer calories.
  • Pregnant or breastfeeding? Your needs are different; ask your doctor.

Frequently asked questions

What is TDEE?

Total daily energy expenditure: the calories you burn in a day including rest, digestion and activity.

How accurate is this calorie calculator?

Formulas like Mifflin-St Jeor are usually within about 10% for most people. Use the result as a starting point and adjust with real results.

Is this medical advice?

No. It is for information only. Talk to a doctor or registered dietitian before starting a diet, especially if you have a health condition.
