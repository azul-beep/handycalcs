How to split a restaurant bill fairly

The food was great, then the check arrives and nobody knows what to do. There is no single right way to split a bill, but there are a few fair ways, and agreeing on one before you order saves a lot of awkward math.

Option 1: Split it evenly

Add tax and tip to the total and divide by the number of people. It is fast and works well when everyone ordered something similar, or when friends go out often and it evens out over time.

Example: a $186 total plus 20% tip ($37.20) is $223.20. For 4 people, that is $55.80 each.

Option 2: Pay for what you ordered

Fairer when orders are very different, such as one person getting a salad and water and another getting steak and two cocktails. Each person adds up their items, then adds their share of tax and tip.

The easy way to do tax and tip: if the tax is 8% and the tip is 20%, multiply each person's subtotal by 1.28. Someone who ordered $32 of food pays $32 × 1.28 = $40.96.

Option 3: Mix the two

Share the appetizers and desserts evenly, and pay your own mains and drinks. This is often the fairest way for groups that shared plates.

What about drinks?

Drinks are where most splits feel unfair. If some people do not drink alcohol, a common solution is to split the food evenly and have each person pay for their own drinks.

Do not forget the tip

The tip should be based on the full bill, not on what is left after someone pays cash. When people pay separately, make sure the total tip still adds up to around 18% to 20%. Many restaurants add an automatic gratuity for large groups, often 6 or more people: check the bill so you do not tip twice.

Ways to make it painless

  • Agree on how to split before ordering, especially with a new group.
  • Ask for separate checks at the start, not at the end, if the restaurant allows it.
  • One person pays, the others send their share by payment app right at the table.
  • Round to the nearest dollar in favor of the person who paid.
