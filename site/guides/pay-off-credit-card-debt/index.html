How to pay off credit card debt faster

Paying only the minimum on a credit card keeps the account in good standing, but it can take many years and cost a lot in interest. A plan, even a simple one, changes that dramatically.

Why the minimum payment takes so long

Minimum payments are often around 1% to 3% of the balance plus the month's interest. As the balance goes down, the minimum goes down too, so the debt shrinks slowly. Credit card interest rates are often above 20% APR, which means a big part of each payment goes to interest, not to the balance.

A quick example

Say you owe $5,000 at 22% APR. The interest alone is about $92 in the first month (5,000 × 0.22 ÷ 12). If you pay a fixed $150 a month, it takes about 52 months (over 4 years) and around $2,800 in interest. Raise it to $300 a month and it takes about 21 months, with roughly $1,000 in interest. Doubling the payment cuts the time by much more than half.

Method 1: Avalanche (saves the most money)

  1. Pay the minimum on every card.
  2. Put every extra dollar on the card with the highest interest rate.
  3. When it is paid off, move that whole payment to the next highest rate.

Mathematically this is the cheapest way, because you kill the most expensive debt first.

Method 2: Snowball (keeps you motivated)

  1. Pay the minimum on every card.
  2. Put every extra dollar on the card with the smallest balance.
  3. When it is gone, roll that payment into the next smallest.

It can cost a bit more in interest, but closing accounts quickly feels great, and many people stick with it longer. The best method is the one you will keep doing.

Other things that help

  • Stop adding new charges to the cards you are paying off. Use a debit card or cash for a while.
  • Ask for a lower rate. A phone call to the card company sometimes works, especially with a good payment history.
  • Balance transfer cards can offer 0% for a period, but there is usually a transfer fee (often 3% to 5%) and the rate jumps when the promo ends. Only worth it with a plan to pay it off in time.
  • Find the extra money with a budget. Even $50 more a month makes a visible difference.
  • Pay more than once a month if it helps you: paying when you get paid lowers the average balance.
