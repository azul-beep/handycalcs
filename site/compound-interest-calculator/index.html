The compound interest formula

For a single deposit: A = P × (1 + r/n)n×t, where P is the deposit, r the annual rate, n how many times a year interest is added and t the years. With monthly contributions each deposit grows for the months left, and the calculator adds them all up month by month.

Example

$5,000 to start plus $200 a month at 7% for 20 years. You put in $53,000 in total and end with about $124,000: more than half of it is interest.

The rule of 72

Divide 72 by the yearly rate to estimate how many years it takes to double your money. At 6% it takes about 12 years; at 9%, about 8.

Where can you get compound interest?

  • High-yield savings accounts and CDs: low risk, FDIC insured up to the limit.
  • Index funds in a 401(k) or IRA: higher long-term returns but the value goes up and down; past returns do not guarantee future ones.
  • Credit cards work the other way: compound interest makes debt grow fast. See the credit card payoff calculator.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between simple and compound interest?

Simple interest is paid only on the original amount. Compound interest is also paid on the interest already earned, so growth speeds up over time.

Does daily compounding make a big difference?

Not much compared with monthly. At 5%, monthly compounding gives an effective 5.12% a year and daily 5.13%.

What rate should I use for the stock market?

Many people use 5% to 7% after inflation for a diversified stock index fund over long periods. It is an estimate, not a promise.
