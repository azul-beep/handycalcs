How it works

Each month the card adds interest (APR ÷ 12 on the balance) and your payment is subtracted. The calculator repeats this until the balance is zero, so you see the exact number of months and the total interest. It assumes you stop using the card while you pay it off.

Example

A $5,000 balance at 22.9% APR with $200 a month takes about 35 months and costs around $1,860 in interest. Paying $300 a month cuts it to 21 months and about $1,080 of interest.

Why the minimum payment is a trap

Minimum payments are usually 1% to 3% of the balance plus interest, so they shrink as the balance shrinks. Paying only the minimum can take more than 10 years. Your statement shows how long it would take; a fixed payment is always faster.

Ways to pay it off faster

  • Avalanche: pay extra on the card with the highest APR first. It saves the most money.
  • Snowball: pay off the smallest balance first for quick wins and motivation.
  • 0% balance transfer: moving the debt to a 0% intro card stops the interest for a while; watch the transfer fee (often 3%–5%).
  • Call your card issuer and ask for a lower rate; it works more often than people think.

Frequently asked questions

How is credit card interest calculated?

Most cards use the average daily balance and a daily rate (APR ÷ 365). Using APR ÷ 12 per month, as this calculator does, gives a very close estimate.

What if my payment is too low?

If the payment is not more than the monthly interest, the balance never goes down. The calculator will warn you.

Should I pay off debt or save first?

Most planners suggest a small emergency fund first (for example $1,000), then attacking high-interest debt, because 20%+ APR is more than savings can earn.
