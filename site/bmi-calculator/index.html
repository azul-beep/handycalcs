The BMI formula

BMI = 703 × weight (lb) ÷ height (in)². Someone 5 ft 7 in (67 inches) who weighs 160 lb has a BMI of 703 × 160 ÷ 4,489 = 25.1.

BMI categories for adults

  • Below 18.5: underweight
  • 18.5 to 24.9: healthy weight
  • 25 to 29.9: overweight
  • 30 or more: obesity

These are the categories used by the CDC and the World Health Organization for adults 20 and older. Children and teens use BMI-for-age percentiles instead.

What BMI does not tell you

BMI is a quick screening number, not a diagnosis. It does not separate muscle from fat, so athletes can show as "overweight", and it does not show where fat is stored. Waist size, blood pressure, blood tests and your doctor's advice give a fuller picture.

Frequently asked questions

What is a healthy BMI?

For adults, 18.5 to 24.9 is considered a healthy weight range.

Is BMI accurate for women, older adults and athletes?

It uses the same formula for everyone, but it can overestimate fat in muscular people and underestimate it in older adults who have lost muscle. Use it as a starting point.

Is this medical advice?

No. It is an informational tool. Talk to a doctor or a registered dietitian about your health and weight.
