To a cat, a Christmas tree is a climbing gym that showed up overnight. To a puppy, it is a pile of new things to chew. You do not have to skip the tree: a few changes make it much safer for everyone.

Pick the spot first

Put the tree in a corner, away from couches, shelves and windowsills that a cat could use as a launch pad. If you can close the door to that room when nobody is home, even better. For very curious pets, a small playpen or baby gate around the base works well.

Anchor it

A tree that tips over can hurt a pet and break everything on it. Use a sturdy, wide stand and tie the top of the tree to a hook in the wall or ceiling with clear fishing line. It takes five minutes and stops most falls.

Ornaments: safe low, fragile high

  • Hang glass, ceramic and sentimental ornaments on the top half of the tree.
  • Use shatterproof, fabric or wood ornaments on the bottom third, where tails and paws reach.
  • Close ornament hooks with pliers, or use ribbon instead of metal hooks.
  • Skip edible ornaments like popcorn strings and candy canes: they invite pets to pull the tree.

Skip the tinsel

Cats love tinsel and ribbon, and swallowed strings can cause a serious intestinal blockage that needs surgery. If you have a cat, leave tinsel, angel hair and loose curling ribbon out this year. Garlands made of thick fabric or wood beads are a safer choice.

Lights and cords

Run cords along the wall, cover them with a cord protector and keep extra wire wrapped tightly. Wrap lights on the inner branches instead of the tips. Unplug the lights when you go out or go to bed: it is safer for pets and for the house.

Cover the tree water

Real trees sit in water that can collect sap, bacteria and any preservatives or fertilizers you add. Use a tree skirt or a stand with a cover so pets cannot drink from it, and keep their own water bowl full and fresh somewhere else.

Real or artificial?

Both can work. Real trees drop needles that can upset stomachs or stick in paws, so sweep often. Artificial trees do not drop needles, but chewed plastic is not great either. For a determined chewer, a smaller tabletop tree out of reach can be the easiest answer.

Give them something better to do

Pets are less interested in the tree when they have their own fun. A new toy, a puzzle feeder or ten minutes of play before you decorate goes a long way. Looking for ideas? See our gift guides for pet lovers, which include gifts for the pets themselves.

General information only. If your pet swallows an ornament, string or tinsel, contact your vet right away.
