Holidays mean full tables, busy kitchens and guests who want to share. Most pet emergencies this time of year start with food left within reach. Here are the ones to know, and what to do if your dog or cat gets into something.

Foods to keep away from pets

  • Chocolate. It contains theobromine and caffeine. Dark and baking chocolate are the most dangerous; milk chocolate is less concentrated but can still make a dog sick. Hot cocoa mix and chocolate-covered treats count too.
  • Xylitol. This sweetener is in sugar-free gum, mints, some peanut butters and some baked goods. In dogs it can cause a fast drop in blood sugar and liver damage. Read labels before you share peanut butter.
  • Grapes, raisins and currants. They can cause kidney failure in dogs, and the amount that causes problems is unpredictable. Watch out for fruitcake, stuffing and trail mix.
  • Onions, garlic, leeks and chives. Raw, cooked or powdered, they damage red blood cells. Cats are even more sensitive than dogs. Gravy, stuffing and many leftovers contain them.
  • Macadamia nuts. They can cause weakness, shaking and fever in dogs.
  • Cooked bones. Turkey and ham bones splinter and can choke or injure the gut. Take the carcass straight to an outside trash can.
  • Fatty scraps. Turkey skin, ham fat and drippings can trigger pancreatitis, a painful illness that often needs a vet visit.
  • Raw yeast dough and alcohol. Dough keeps rising in the stomach and produces alcohol. Unattended drinks are a risk for curious pets too.

Plants to watch

Lilies are the big one for cats. Every part of a true lily, even the pollen or the water in the vase, can cause kidney failure in cats. If you have a cat, leave lilies out of holiday bouquets. Poinsettias, mistletoe and holly usually cause stomach upset rather than serious poisoning, but it is still best to keep them out of reach.

What to do if your pet eats something

  1. Take whatever is left away from your pet and keep the package: the label helps the vet.
  2. Do not make your pet vomit unless a vet tells you to.
  3. Call your vet, an emergency clinic or a pet poison line right away, even if your pet seems fine. Some poisonings take hours to show signs.

Two animal poison lines in the US are open 24 hours: the ASPCA Animal Poison Control Center at (888) 426-4435 and the Pet Poison Helpline at (855) 764-7661. Both may charge a consultation fee. Save one of them in your phone before the holidays start.

Easy ways to prevent it

  • Ask guests not to feed your pet, and tell kids why.
  • Keep gifts with food inside away from the tree: dogs can smell chocolate through wrapping paper.
  • Give your pet their own treat or a stuffed chew toy while you eat.
  • Set up a quiet room for pets when the house is full.
