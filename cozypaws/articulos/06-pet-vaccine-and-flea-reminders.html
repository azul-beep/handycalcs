"When was the last flea dose?" is one of the most common questions in pet households. Vaccines, monthly pills and yearly checkups are easy to lose track of, especially with more than one pet. A simple system fixes that.

What to keep track of

  • Vaccines: the name, the date given and the date the next one is due (it is on the vet's receipt or certificate).
  • Flea and tick prevention: the product and the date of each dose. Many are monthly, some last 8 or 12 weeks.
  • Heartworm prevention: usually monthly. The American Heartworm Society recommends giving it all year.
  • Deworming for puppies and kittens, as your vet schedules it.
  • Checkups, dental cleanings and lab work.
  • Weight, because medication doses depend on it.

Typical vaccine schedules (ask your vet for yours)

Dogs: the core vaccines are rabies and the combination shot for distemper, parvovirus and adenovirus (often called DHPP or DAPP). Puppies usually get a series every 3 to 4 weeks until about 16 weeks old, a booster around one year, and then boosters every 1 to 3 years. Others, like Bordetella (kennel cough), leptospirosis or Lyme, depend on where you live and what your dog does.

Cats: the core vaccines are rabies and FVRCP (feline herpesvirus, calicivirus and panleukopenia). Kittens get a series, then boosters. Feline leukemia (FeLV) is recommended for kittens and for cats that go outside.

Rabies rules are set by state and local law, so the timing for your pet may be 1 or 3 years. Your vet knows the rules where you live.

Set reminders that actually work

  1. Write the next due date the same day you get the vaccine or give the pill, not later.
  2. Use one place for all your pets: a notebook on the fridge, a calendar or an app.
  3. Set the reminder a few days early for anything that needs an appointment.
  4. Give monthly meds on an easy-to-remember date, like the 1st of the month.
  5. Keep a photo of each vaccine certificate on your phone for groomers, boarding and travel.
