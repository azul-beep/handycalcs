Short days and closed windows mean less to watch, less sun and often less movement. Some cats get bored, some gain weight, and some start waking you up at 4 AM. A few small changes help a lot.

Two short play sessions a day

Most cats do best with short, intense play: 10 to 15 minutes, twice a day. Use a wand toy and move it like prey, hiding behind furniture and then dashing away. Let your cat "catch" it at the end, then give a small meal or treat. Hunt, catch, eat, sleep is the natural rhythm, and it often helps with night-time zoomies too.

Give them a window

A window perch or a chair by a sunny window is cat TV. A bird feeder outside the window (where cats cannot reach the birds) makes it even better.

Make food a game

Puzzle feeders, treat balls or a few pieces of kibble hidden around the house turn a meal into an activity. Start easy so your cat does not give up, then make it harder.

Warm spots

Cats love warmth. A bed in a sunny spot, a soft blanket on a favorite chair or a covered bed away from drafts all help, especially for senior cats with stiff joints. Be careful with space heaters and fireplaces: use guards, and never leave them on with no one home.

Water matters in winter too

Heating dries the air, and many cats do not drink much on their own. Try a water fountain, a few bowls in different rooms, or adding some wet food to the diet.

Watch the weight

Less activity with the same amount of food usually means extra weight. Measure food instead of free-feeding, count treats, and weigh your cat once a month. You should be able to feel the ribs and see a waist.

A safety note for neighborhood cats

Outdoor and stray cats sometimes sleep near warm car engines in cold weather. Knocking on the hood or honking before you start the car on a cold morning can save a life.
