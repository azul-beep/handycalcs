The first week with a puppy is exciting, loud and a little chaotic. A bit of planning makes it much easier for both of you. Here is what to have ready and what to focus on day by day.

Before the puppy comes home

  • Basics: food (start with what the breeder or shelter used), food and water bowls, collar or harness, ID tag, leash.
  • A crate or a pen the right size, with a soft bed or blanket.
  • Chew toys of different textures. Puppies chew a lot while their teeth come in.
  • Cleaning supplies: an enzyme cleaner for accidents, paper towels, poop bags.
  • Puppy-proofing: lift cords, shoes, socks, kids' toys, houseplants and anything with medicine or chemicals. Get down to puppy height and look around.
  • A vet appointment in the first few days, and the puppy's records from the breeder or shelter.

Days 1 and 2: keep it calm

Let your puppy explore one or two rooms first, not the whole house. Keep visitors to a minimum. Puppies sleep a lot (often 18 hours or more a day), so give plenty of quiet nap time. Show the water bowl, the bed and the potty spot right away.

Potty training from the first hour

Take your puppy out to the same spot:

  • first thing in the morning and last thing at night,
  • after every meal and drink,
  • after waking up from a nap,
  • after playing,
  • and every 1 to 2 hours in between while awake.

Praise and treat right after your puppy goes. Accidents will happen: clean them with an enzyme cleaner and do not punish, which only teaches puppies to hide. Young puppies often need one trip out during the night.

Nights

Many puppies cry the first nights away from their littermates. Putting the crate next to your bed helps a lot, and so does a blanket that smells like their old home. A short potty trip at night is fine; keep it boring and go straight back to bed.

The first vet visit

Bring the vaccine and deworming records. Your vet will do a checkup, plan the vaccine series and talk about flea, tick and heartworm prevention, microchipping and when to spay or neuter. Write down every date you get.

Start socializing (safely)

Puppies learn the most about the world before about 14 to 16 weeks of age. Gently introduce new sounds, surfaces, people and handling (paws, ears, mouth) with lots of treats. Ask your vet when it is safe to meet other dogs and visit public places, since the vaccine series is not finished yet.
