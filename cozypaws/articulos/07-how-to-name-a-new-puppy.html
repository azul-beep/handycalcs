You will say your pet's name thousands of times: at the park, at the vet, from the back door at night. A good name is easy to say, easy for your pet to hear, and makes you smile. Here is how to find one.

1. Keep it short

Names with one or two syllables are quick to call and easy for pets to pick out of everyday talk. A longer name is fine too, as long as it has a short version you will really use (Sir Reginald Barksworth can be "Reggie").

2. Use sounds pets notice

Many trainers suggest names with a clear, crisp start (like K, T, B or P sounds) and names that end in a vowel sound, like "Milo", "Luna" or "Coco". They are easy to say with an upbeat tone, which pets respond to.

3. Avoid names that sound like commands

"Kit" sounds like "sit", "Bo" sounds like "no" and "Ray" sounds like "stay". Also avoid names that sound like other people or pets in the house. Confused pets learn more slowly.

4. Do the park test

Say the name out loud, then yell it like you are calling your dog at a busy park. If you would feel silly shouting it, keep looking.

5. Think about personality, looks or what you love

  • Looks: Pepper, Biscuit, Mocha, Shadow, Ginger.
  • Food: Noodle, Waffles, Olive, Nacho, Peanut.
  • Seasons and holidays: Holly, Jingle, Frost, Cocoa, Juniper.
  • Nature: River, Aspen, Willow, Sky, Maple.
  • Classic people names: Charlie, Daisy, Oliver, Rosie, Max.
  • Spanish names: Canela, Chispa, Lucero, Pancho, Paloma.

6. Let the family vote

Make a short list of 5 to 10 names and spend a couple of days trying them. Often one name simply "sticks" when you look at your new pet.

7. Teach the name with treats

Say the name in a happy voice, and the moment your pet looks at you, give a treat. Repeat a few times a day for short sessions. Never use the name to scold. Changing the name in the first weeks is fine: young pets learn a new name quickly.
