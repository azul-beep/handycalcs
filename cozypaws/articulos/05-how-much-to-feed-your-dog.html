The feeding chart on the bag is a starting point, not a rule. It is written for an "average" dog, and most dogs are not average. Here is how to find a better number for your own dog, in three steps.

Step 1: Resting energy (RER)

Vets often start with the Resting Energy Requirement, the calories a dog burns just to stay alive at rest:

RER = 70 × (body weight in kg)0.75

To get kg, divide pounds by 2.2. For example, a 44 lb dog weighs 20 kg, and 200.75 is about 9.46, so the RER is about 70 × 9.46 = 662 calories a day.

Step 2: Multiply by a life-stage factor

Your dogMultiply RER by
Adult, spayed or neutered1.6
Adult, not neutered1.8
Senior or not very active1.4
Needs to lose weight (at target weight)1.0
Very active or working2 to 5
Puppy2 to 3

Our 20 kg neutered adult would need about 662 × 1.6 = 1,059 calories a day.

Step 3: Turn calories into cups

Look for "kcal per cup" on the bag (it is usually near the feeding guide). If the food has 370 kcal per cup, our dog needs about 1,059 ÷ 370 = 2.9 cups a day, split into two meals. Use a real measuring cup, or better, a kitchen scale: a "cup" scooped with a random mug can be off by a lot.

Do not forget treats

Treats, chews and table scraps add up fast. Keep them under about 10% of daily calories, and cut the meal a little on days with lots of training treats.

Check the dog, not just the math

Every formula is an estimate. The best check is your dog's body:

  • You should feel the ribs easily with light pressure, without a thick layer of fat.
  • From above, you should see a waist behind the ribs.
  • From the side, the belly should tuck up behind the chest.

Weigh your dog every 2 to 4 weeks. If the weight goes up, cut the food by about 10% and check again in a few weeks. If it goes down without trying, talk to your vet.
