Cooking for 12 instead of 4, or halving a cake recipe for two? Scaling a recipe is simple math, but a few things (eggs, pans, baking time) need a little extra care.

Step 1: Find the scaling factor

Factor = servings you want ÷ servings in the recipe

A recipe for 4 that you want to make for 10: 10 ÷ 4 = 2.5. Multiply every ingredient by 2.5. For half a recipe, the factor is 0.5.

Step 2: Convert awkward amounts

Multiplying gives odd numbers like "2.5 × ⅓ cup". Converting to smaller units makes it easier to measure:

UnitEquals
1 cup16 tablespoons = 48 teaspoons
¼ cup4 tablespoons
⅓ cup5 tablespoons + 1 teaspoon
1 tablespoon3 teaspoons
1 cup (US)about 240 ml
1 stick of butter½ cup = 8 tablespoons = 113 g

So 2.5 × ⅓ cup = ⅚ cup, which is ½ cup + ⅓ cup, or 13 tablespoons + 1 teaspoon.

Weigh when you can

A kitchen scale makes scaling exact. A cup of flour can weigh anywhere from about 115 to 150 grams depending on how you scoop it, while 125 g is always 125 g. Many baking recipes give grams for this reason.

Eggs

A large egg is about 50 g without the shell (about 3 tablespoons). If the math says 1.5 eggs, beat 2 eggs and use about three quarters of it, or use 1 egg plus 1 to 2 tablespoons of beaten egg.

Seasoning, leavening and liquids

  • Salt and spices: when scaling up a lot, add about 75% first, taste, then add more.
  • Baking powder and soda: scale exactly for small changes. For very large batches, bake in several normal-size batches instead.
  • Liquids in soups and stews: evaporation does not grow at the same rate in a wider pot, so you may need a little less liquid.

Pans and baking time

When you scale a cake or a casserole, the pan matters. Compare pan areas: a 9×13 inch pan (117 sq in) holds about the same as two 8-inch square pans (64 sq in each, 128 total). A 9-inch round pan is about 64 sq in. If the batter ends up deeper than the original, lower the oven by about 25°F and bake longer; if it is shallower, start checking early.

Oven temperatures

°C = (°F − 32) × 5 ÷ 9. 350°F is about 175 to 180°C, 400°F is 200°C, and 425°F is about 220°C. Recipes from the UK may use gas marks: gas mark 4 is about 350°F.
