Compound interest is interest that earns interest. It starts slow and then speeds up, which is why time matters more than almost anything else when you save, and why debt can grow so fast.

Simple vs. compound interest

With simple interest, you earn interest only on the money you put in. $1,000 at 5% simple interest earns $50 every year: $1,500 after 10 years.

With compound interest, each year's interest is added to the balance, and next year you earn interest on the bigger amount. $1,000 at 5% compounded yearly grows to about $1,629 after 10 years. Same rate, $129 more, just from compounding.

The formula

A = P × (1 + r ÷ n)n × t

  • A is the final amount
  • P is the starting amount (principal)
  • r is the yearly rate as a decimal (5% = 0.05)
  • n is how many times a year interest is added (12 for monthly)
  • t is the number of years

Example: $1,000 at 7% for 10 years, compounded once a year, becomes about $1,967. Compounded monthly, it becomes about $2,010. More frequent compounding helps a little, but the rate and the time matter much more.

Time is the secret ingredient

Look at what $1,000 becomes at 7% a year, compounded yearly:

10$1,967

40$14,974

The last 10 years add more than the first 30 combined. That is why starting early, even with small amounts, makes such a difference.

Adding money every month

Regular deposits make compounding much stronger. Saving $200 a month at a 7% yearly return for 30 years adds up to $72,000 of your own money, but the balance would be roughly $240,000, because most of the growth comes from interest on interest. Real investment returns go up and down from year to year, so treat any fixed rate as an estimate, not a promise.

The rule of 72

To estimate how long it takes to double your money, divide 72 by the yearly rate. At 6%, money doubles in about 12 years (72 ÷ 6). At 9%, about 8 years.

It works against you too

Credit cards compound as well, usually at rates above 20%. By the rule of 72, an unpaid balance at 24% would double in about 3 years if you made no payments. Paying off high-interest debt is like getting a guaranteed return equal to its rate.
