If budgeting apps and spreadsheets feel like too much, the 50/30/20 rule is a simple place to start. It splits your take-home pay into three buckets, and you only need to watch three numbers.

How it works

  • 50% for needs: rent or mortgage, utilities, groceries, insurance, transportation to work, minimum loan and card payments, child care.
  • 30% for wants: eating out, streaming, hobbies, travel, shopping that is not essential, upgrades like a nicer phone plan.
  • 20% for savings and debt: emergency fund, retirement, and any debt payments above the minimum.

It uses take-home pay: what lands in your account after taxes and paycheck deductions. If your employer takes out retirement or health insurance before you get paid, you can add those back to your savings and needs buckets.

Needs or wants?

This is where most people get stuck. A simple test: would you still have to pay it if you lost your job tomorrow? Groceries are a need; takeout is a want. A basic phone plan is a need; the newest phone is a want. Be honest, because putting wants in the needs bucket hides the problem.

When 50% is not enough for needs

In many cities, rent alone can take 40% of take-home pay. The rule still helps, as a direction:

  • Try 60/20/20 or 60/30/10 for now, and write down when you want to get back to 50%.
  • Look at the big three first: housing, transportation and food. Small cuts there beat cancelling every small subscription.
  • Keep at least something in savings each month, even if it is 5%. The habit matters.

Where the 20% goes first

  1. A small emergency fund (many people aim for $1,000 to start).
  2. Get any employer retirement match: it is free money.
  3. Pay down high-interest debt, like credit cards.
  4. Build the emergency fund up to 3 to 6 months of needs.
  5. Save for goals and invest for retirement.
